How to play Ntuxa

The board

The board has four rows of bins (4 × 16 by default; 4 × 8 up to 4 × 24 boards are traditional). Each player owns the two rows on their side: the attacking row in the middle of the board and the back row at the edge. At the start every bin holds two seeds. South moves first.

A turn

  1. Choose one of your non-empty bins.
  2. Remove all the seeds from that bin.
  3. Drop them one by one into the following bins, anti-clockwise, around your own two rows.
  4. If the last seed lands in a bin that was not empty, pick up all the seeds in that bin and continue sowing (a relay). If it lands in an empty bin, the sowing ends.

Capturing

When the sowing ends in an empty bin of your attacking row and the facing bin in your opponent's attacking row (same column) has seeds, you capture all of their seeds in that column (both their attacking and back bins). Captured seeds are removed from the game. Ending in your back row, or facing an empty attacking bin, simply ends your turn, even if their back bin has seeds.

Optional extra bin rule: before a game you may choose to also take one extra bin of your choice from the opponent's side after every capture. Online, both players must pick the same rule.

Two phases

Phase one: while you have any bin with more than one seed, you may not play a bin holding a single seed.

Phase two: once every non-empty bin on your side holds exactly one seed, you may play any single seed whose next bin is empty.

End of the game

You win by capturing all of your opponent's seeds.

Clarifications used in this version

  • A sowing long enough to go all the way around also drops a seed into the bin it started from.
  • If a player still has seeds but no legal move (only possible in phase two when no single seed has an empty bin ahead of it), that player loses.
  • A safety limit stops a single turn after 20,000 sown seeds, in case of an endless relay loop.
  • An optional move limit (off by default) can end very long games; the player with more seeds then wins.

About Ntuxa

Ntxuva is a traditional mancala game from Mozambique and belongs to the four-row ("Mancala IV") family that is believed to have originated in East Africa, alongside games such as Bao (Zanzibar), Igisoro (Rwanda) and Omweso (Uganda). It is called Ntxuva in the south of Mozambique and Mthadje or M'pale in the north.

It is played on carved wooden boards, or in holes dug in the ground, with seeds or small stones. Boards range from 4 × 8 to 4 × 24 bins and are always split evenly between the two players.

This version is built with Three.js and runs entirely in your browser. All graphics and sounds are generated in code. The rules follow the description provided for this project. See Learn the Rules for the details used where traditions differ.

Controls

  • Click / tap a bin to play it.
  • Drag to orbit, scroll / pinch to zoom, right-drag / two fingers to pan.
  • Arrow keys move the keyboard cursor, Enter plays the bin.
  • Space or a click during an animation fast-forwards it.
  • U or Ctrl+Z undo · H hint (practice) · C reset the camera · Esc pause.
